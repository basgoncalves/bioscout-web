# Is there a minimum "tip-toe" capacity required before running 5 / 10 / 20 km?

Short evidence review — B. Gonçalves, 8 Sep 2026

---

## 1. Question

**P** — adults returning to, or starting, distance running
**I/E** — performance on a "tip-toe" test: single-leg heel-rise (calf-raise) repetitions,
sustained toe-walking/standing time, or repeated tip-toe (pogo) hops
**C** — lower vs higher test performance, or pass vs fail of a stated threshold
**O** — ability to complete 5 km / 10 km / 20 km of running without injury or symptom flare

> Is there peer-reviewed evidence for a **minimum number of minutes or tip-toe jumps**
> that must be achieved before a person can run these distances?

## 2. Bottom line

**No.** No peer-reviewed study establishes a tip-toe threshold — in repetitions, seconds,
or hops — that predicts the ability to run any given distance. The evidence base contains:

1. good **descriptive/normative** data on the calf-raise test,
2. **expert-opinion criteria** (Delphi, clinical commentaries, protocol papers) that
   propose thresholds without validating them against running outcomes, and
3. **no prospective study** that pass/fail-classifies runners on a tip-toe test and then
   measures whether they tolerate 5/10/20 km.

The distance-specific numbers circulating in practice (e.g. "25–30 single-leg heel raises",
"30 s of pain-free hopping", "20 pogo hops") are **consensus artefacts, not validated
cut-offs**. They should be described as clinical thresholds of convenience, not as evidence-based
prerequisites for a running distance.

## 3. What the literature does contain

### 3.1 Normative and measurement work (strongest evidence)

- Hébert-Losier et al. (2009), *J Sci Med Sport* — systematic review, "Raising the standards
  of the calf-raise test". Found extreme heterogeneity in protocols (cadence, range, knee angle,
  support, endpoint definition), making cross-study thresholds non-comparable.
- Hébert-Losier et al. (2017), *Physiotherapy* — updated reliability and normative values for the
  standing heel-rise test in healthy adults. Repetition counts decline markedly with age and
  differ by sex; a single universal cut-off is not defensible.
- Calf Raise Application normative study (2025), *Braz J Phys Ther* — large international
  cross-sectional normative dataset. Descriptive only; no link to running outcomes.
- Calf muscle strength-endurance in Western Australian Football players (2023), *J Sci Med Sport*
  — shows that standardisation of the SLHR protocol materially changes the score.

> **Implication:** the test is reliable *when standardised*, but the literature defines what is
> *typical*, never what is *sufficient*.

### 3.2 Return-to-running criteria (weak evidence)

- **Tibial bone stress injury scoping review** (Sports Med, 2024; PMID 39141251). 50 studies,
  39 of which were reviews/commentaries, only 2 RCTs. The authors conclude there is
  "a lack of consistency or strong evidence to guide this process", and that recommendations
  rest on **level IV evidence**. Calf/heel raises are not even reported as a criterion; the
  single-leg hop appears in only 8% of studies. Progression is symptom-guided walk–run, with
  **no defined distance milestones**.
- **Achilles tendon rupture return-to-running Delphi consensus** (2026, PMID 42503592) —
  criteria are agreed by expert panel, i.e. consensus, not predictive validation.
- Sport-specific return-to-running frameworks (e.g. JOSPT Open 2025, professional football)
  propose calf-raise and hopping entry criteria, again as expert-informed thresholds.

### 3.3 Why the construct does not transfer

A mechanistic reason the mapping is unlikely to exist as a simple threshold:

- A heel-rise test is a **slow, largely concentric, low-rate** task; running plantarflexion is a
  **stretch-shortening cycle at ~2.5–3 Hz** with Achilles tendon forces in the region of
  ~6–8× body weight per step, plus substantial elastic energy contribution
  (Nature *Sci Rep* 2021; cumulative-load work, e.g. PMID 31985576).
- Tolerance of 5 vs 20 km is dominated by **cumulative load** (step count × per-step force),
  which scales with distance, cadence, speed and body mass — variables the tip-toe test does
  not sample at all.
- So even a validated threshold would have to be distance- *and* speed- *and* mass-specific.
  Nothing of that kind has been tested.

## 4. What can be said honestly

| Claim | Support |
|---|---|
| Calf-raise/hop tests are reliable and have normative data | Good |
| Low calf capacity is associated with Achilles/calf pathology | Moderate (cross-sectional) |
| Calf capacity should be restored before returning to running | Expert consensus, level IV–V |
| A specific rep/time/hop count is required before 5 km | **No evidence** |
| That count differs for 5 vs 10 vs 20 km | **No evidence; not studied** |

## 5. Pragmatic thresholds by distance — with evidence grading

> **Read the grading column before the numbers.** Nothing below was derived from a study that
> tested a threshold against a running-distance outcome, because no such study exists (§2).
> These are **consensus thresholds of convenience**, assembled from expert-opinion return-to-running
> frameworks and normative data, and the *distance gradient* in particular (why 20 km should need
> more than 5 km) is an assumption, not a finding. They are defensible as a clinical starting point
> and as prompts for progressive loading; they are **not** defensible as prerequisites, gates, or
> claims about who "can" run a distance.

Grading uses a GRADE-style descriptor for the specific claim *"this value discriminates readiness
for this distance"* — not for the test's reliability, which is separately good.

### 5.1 Calf / plantarflexor capacity

| Test (standardised protocol) | 5 km | 10 km | 20 km | Basis | Quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-leg heel raise, reps to failure (~30/min metronome, full range, fingertip support) | ≥ 20–25 | ≥ 25 | ≥ 25–30 | Normative data (age/sex adjusted) + RTR consensus | **Very low** — norms are descriptive; no outcome validation |
| SL heel-raise limb symmetry index | ≥ 90 % | ≥ 90 % | ≥ 90 % | Borrowed from RTS literature | **Very low** (indirect; LSI evidence sits in ACL, not running) |
| Sustained bilateral tip-toe hold (heels up, still) | 30 s | 45 s | 60 s | Expert opinion only | **Very low** |
| Continuous tip-toe walking | ~1 min | ~2 min | ~2–3 min | Expert opinion only | **Very low** — no study reports this test at all |

### 5.2 Tip-toe hopping / stretch-shortening capacity

| Test (pain-free, ~2.2–2.5 Hz, stiff ankle, minimal knee flexion) | 5 km | 10 km | 20 km | Basis | Quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Double-leg pogo hops, continuous reps | 30–40 | ~50 | 60+ | Expert opinion / clinical protocols | **Very low** |
| Single-leg pogo hops, continuous reps | 20–25 | 25–30 | 30+ | Expert opinion / clinical protocols | **Very low** |
| Continuous single-leg hopping, duration | 30 s | 45 s | 60 s | Expert opinion / clinical protocols | **Very low** |
| Single-leg hop for distance, LSI | ≥ 90 % | ≥ 90 % | ≥ 90 % | Only functional test with signal in the tibial BSI review (1 RCT, 8 % of studies) | **Low** — the least unsupported row in this table |

### 5.3 Symptom and load-response criteria (better supported than any rep count)

| Criterion | 5 km | 10 km | 20 km | Quality of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pain during test/run ≤ 2/10 | same | same | same | **Low–moderate** (tendinopathy load-monitoring literature) |
| Symptoms settle within 24 h, no next-morning stiffness increase | same | same | same | **Low–moderate** |
| Tolerated the preceding walk–run progression step without flare | the actual gate at every distance | | | **Low** — symptom-guided graded progression is the one consistently endorsed approach |

**How to use this in practice or in text:** the symptom/load-response rows (§5.3) and the graded
walk–run progression carry more support than any number in §5.1–5.2. If a threshold in §5.1–5.2 is
cited, it should be attributed as expert consensus and accompanied by the grading, not presented as
an evidence-based minimum.

## 6. Research gap (worth writing up)

The question is answerable and unclaimed. A minimal design:

- Prospective cohort of runners returning to running (or novices), baseline standardised
  SLHR (Calf Raise App), sustained toe-walk time, and 30 s repeated tip-toe hop test
  (contact time, RSI).
- Outcome: highest completed continuous distance without symptom flare over 12 weeks,
  plus GPS/watch-derived cumulative load.
- Analysis: ROC / conditional-inference tree for a distance-specific cut-off; report
  sensitivity, specificity and — critically — the calibration, since the whole point is
  whether a threshold carries any predictive information at all.

Feasible with the Calf Raise App + a consumer watch; low cost, clinically wanted.

## 7. Search notes

Web-based search, Sep 2026 (PubMed/ScienceDirect/Springer via web). Terms combined:
*calf raise test / single-leg heel rise / heel-raise*, *pogo hop / repeated hopping*,
*return to running criteria*, *normative values*, *plantarflexor endurance*,
*running distance tolerance / cumulative load*.
No study was retrieved that tested a tip-toe threshold against a running-distance outcome.
**This review is a rapid scan, not a systematic search** — no protocol registration, no
dual screening, no grey-literature search. Treat the "no evidence" conclusion as
"no evidence found under these terms", and confirm with a proper PubMed/Embase/SPORTDiscus
strategy before publishing the claim.
